
***INGREDIENTS:***
 - Bife de Frango (Chicken breast fillets): 2-3 pieces, sliced into bite-sized strips
- Massa (Pasta): Your choice of pasta shape (Penne or Fusilli work beautifully here)
- Cebola (White onion): (1/2 C) chopped finely
- Alho (Garlic): 2 cloves, minced
- Polpa de Tomate (Tomato purée): 4-5 tablespoons
- Vinho Verde: A generous splash (1/4 C)
- Manteiga (Butter): 1 tablespoon
- Pimentão Doce (Sweet paprika): 1 teaspoon
- Queijo Ralado (Shredded cheese): A handful for melting
- Salt, pepper, and garlic powder to taste

***DIRECTIONS***:
Prep the Pasta: Bring a pot of salted water to a boil on your stove and cook the pasta until al dente. Drain and set aside, tossing with a tiny bit of butter so it doesn't stick.

Sear the Chicken: Season your chicken strips with salt, pepper, garlic powder, and sweet paprika. Heat a large skillet over medium-high heat with a splash of olive oil (or butter). Sear the chicken until golden brown on both sides. Remove from the pan and set aside (it doesn't need to be fully cooked through yet).

Build the Refogado (Base): Turn the heat down to medium. In the same pan, add the butter, chopped white onion. Sauté for 3–4 minutes until softened. Add the minced garlic and cook for 1 more minute until fragrant.

Deglaze and Simmer: Pour in the Vinho Verde, using a wooden spoon to scrape up all those delicious browned bits from the bottom of the pan. Let the alcohol bubble away for 2 minutes. Stir in the polpa de tomate.

Combine: Return the chicken and any resting juices back to the pan. Turn the heat to low, cover, and let it simmer for 5 minutes until the chicken is cooked through. Stir in the cooked pasta to coat it in the sauce.

The Cheese Finish: Turn off the heat. Scatter your queijo ralado generously over the top. Put a lid on the pan for 2 minutes—the residual heat from the stove will melt the cheese into a glorious, gooey blanket.

Chef's Tip: In Portugal, a refogado (onion, garlic, and fat base) is the holy trinity of cooking. To get the most out of your convection stove, don't rush the onions. Let them get soft and translucent before adding the garlic so the garlic doesn't burn and turn bitter.
