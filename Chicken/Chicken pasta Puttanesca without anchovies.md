
***INGREDIENTS***:
 * **Massa** (Pasta of choice, e.g., Spaghetti or Penne) – 200g
 * **Carne picada de frango (Ground chicken) – 300g *(Note: Traditional puttanesca doesn't have meat, but we are making it a hearty dinner!)*
 * **Polpa de tomate** (Tomato purée) – 1 can/pack (approx. 400g)
 * **Alho** (Garlic) – 3-4 cloves, thinly sliced
 * **Echalotas** (Shallots) or **Cebola** (Onion) – 1 small, finely diced
 * **Azeitonas pretas** (Black olives) – ½ cup, pitted and chopped *(Look for the local 'Galega' olives for a distinct, slightly fruity Portuguese twist)*
 * **Alcaparras** (Capers) – 2 tablespoons, drained and rinsed
 * **Piri-piri** (Portuguese hot sauce or dried chili flakes) – To taste
 * **Vinho verde** – A generous splash (around ¼ cup) to deglaze
 * **Azeite** (Olive oil), salt, and black pepper

***DIRECTIONS:***
 1. **Boil the Pasta:** Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook your *massa* according to the package instructions until *al dente*. Drain, reserving a splash of pasta water.
 2. **Brown the frango** Heat a splash of *azeite* in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the *carne frango*, seasoning it with salt, pepper, and a pinch of garlic powder. Cook until deeply browned, breaking it up with your spoon. Remove the chicken from the pan and set aside (we want to keep those tasty brown bits in the pan).
 3. **Build the Base:** Turn the heat down to medium. Add a bit more olive oil to the same pan. Sauté your diced shallots (or onion) and sliced garlic until soft and fragrant (about 2-3 minutes). Add a dash of *piri-piri* for a kick.
 4. **Deglaze:** Pour in the *vinho verde*, using your spoon to scrape up all the caramelized beef bits from the bottom of the pan. Let it simmer for 1-2 minutes until the alcohol smell cooks off.
 5. **Simmer the Sauce:** Stir in the *polpa de tomate*, chopped black olives, and capers. Return the browned beef to the skillet. Turn the heat to low and let it simmer for about 10 minutes so the flavors marry.
 6. **Toss and Serve:** Add the cooked pasta directly into the sauce. Toss well, adding a splash of your reserved pasta water if the sauce needs loosening.
> ### 👩‍🍳 Chef's Tip
> Because we left out the anchovies, the salt balance shifts. Olives and capers are naturally very salty, so **do not add salt to the sauce** until the very end after you've tasted it! If it feels like it's missing depth, a tiny drop of yellow mustard or a pinch of smoked paprika (*pimento doce fumado*) can mimic that missing savory complexity.
